## Overview of I²C

I²C (Inter‑Integrated Circuit) is a synchronous, multi‑master, multi‑slave serial bus used for short‑distance communication between integrated circuits on a PCB. It uses **two wires**:

| Line | Function |
|------|----------|
| **SDA** | Serial Data – carries the data bits |
| **SCL** | Serial Clock – generated by the master to time the transfer |

Both lines are **open‑drain** (or open‑collector) and require pull‑up resistors to a supply voltage (typically 3.3 V or 5 V).

---

## Communication Phases

1. **Start Condition**  
   The master pulls **SDA** low while **SCL** remains high. This signals all slaves that a transfer is beginning.

2. **Address Frame**  
   The master sends a 7‑bit (or 10‑bit) slave address followed by a **R/W** bit (0 = write, 1 = read).  
   - All slaves compare the address; only the matching slave acknowledges (ACK) by pulling SDA low during the 9th clock pulse.

3. **Data Transfer**  
   - **Write**: Master sends 8‑bit data bytes; each byte is followed by an ACK from the slave.  
   - **Read**: Slave sends data bytes; the master ACKs each byte except the last one, where it sends a NACK to signal the end of the read.

4. **Stop Condition**  
   The master releases SDA to high while SCL is high, ending the communication.

---

## Timing Details

- **Clock Stretching**: A slave may hold SCL low to delay the master if it needs more time to process data. The master must wait until SCL is released.
- **Bus Speed Modes**  
  - **Standard‑mode**: up to 100 kHz  
  - **Fast‑mode**: up to 400 kHz  
  - **Fast‑mode plus**: up to 1 MHz  
  - **High‑speed mode**: up to 3.4 MHz (requires additional start/stop handling)

---

## Typical Use Cases

- Connecting sensors (temperature, accelerometer) to microcontrollers.  
- Communicating with EEPROMs, RTCs, LCD controllers, and GPIO expanders.  
- Multi‑device setups where a single master controls several peripherals on the same bus.

---

## Example Transaction (Master writes a byte)

1. **START** → SDA low while SCL high.  
2. **Address + Write** → 0x50 (7‑bit address) + 0 → 0xA0.  
3. **ACK** from slave.  
4. **Data Byte** → 0x37.  
5. **ACK** from slave.  
6. **STOP** → SDA released high while SCL high.
