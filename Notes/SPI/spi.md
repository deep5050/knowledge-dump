### 🔧 **Key Components of SPI**

1. **MOSI (Master Out Slave In)**  
   - Data line from master to slave.

2. **MISO (Master In Slave Out)**  
   - Data line from slave to master.

3. **SCLK (Serial Clock)**  
   - Clock signal generated by the master to synchronize data transfer.

4. **SS/CS (Slave Select / Chip Select)**  
   - Active-low signal used by the master to select which slave to communicate with.

---

### 🔁 **How SPI Works**

- The **master** initiates communication by pulling the **CS** line of a slave **low**.
- It then sends clock pulses on **SCLK**.
- Data is shifted out on **MOSI** and simultaneously read in on **MISO** (full-duplex).
- After the transfer, the master pulls **CS high** to deselect the slave.

---

### 📦 **Data Transfer Characteristics**

- **Full-duplex**: Data can be sent and received at the same time.
- **Synchronous**: Data is transferred in sync with the clock.
- **No addressing**: Each slave needs a separate CS line.
- **Configurable**: Clock polarity (CPOL) and phase (CPHA) can be adjusted.

---

### 📊 **SPI Modes (0–3)**

SPI has 4 modes based on CPOL and CPHA:

| Mode | CPOL | CPHA | Clock Idle State | Data Captured On |
|------|------|------|------------------|------------------|
| 0    | 0    | 0    | Low              | Rising edge      |
| 1    | 0    | 1    | Low              | Falling edge     |
| 2    | 1    | 0    | High             | Falling edge     |
| 3    | 1    | 1    | High             | Rising edge      |

---

### ✅ **Advantages**

- High-speed data transfer.
- Simple hardware interface.
- Full-duplex communication.

### ❌ **Disadvantages**

- Requires more pins (especially with multiple slaves).
- No built-in error checking or acknowledgment.
- No standard for data framing or flow control.


### 🔁 **Basic Comparison**

| Feature              | **I2C**                              | **SPI**                              |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Wires Required**   | 2 (SDA, SCL)                         | 4 (MOSI, MISO, SCLK, SS/CS)          |
| **Speed**            | Up to ~3.4 Mbps (High-Speed Mode)   | Up to tens of Mbps (faster)          |
| **Communication**    | Half-duplex                         | Full-duplex                          |
| **Master/Slave**     | Multi-master supported               | Single master (typically)            |
| **Addressing**       | Uses 7-bit or 10-bit addresses       | No addressing; uses chip select lines|
| **Complexity**       | More complex (protocol overhead)     | Simpler protocol                     |
| **Data Integrity**   | Built-in ACK/NACK, start/stop bits   | No built-in error checking           |
| **Distance**         | Better for short distances           | Better for very short distances      |
| **Power Consumption**| Lower (fewer lines, slower speed)    | Higher (more lines, faster speed)    |

---

### 🧠 **When to Use What?**

- **Use I2C when:**
  - You have many devices and limited GPIOs.
  - You need moderate speed and simplicity in wiring.
  - Power consumption is a concern.

- **Use SPI when:**
  - You need high-speed communication.
  - You have fewer devices or enough GPIOs for chip select lines.
  - You want full-duplex data transfer.

---

### 🛠️ **Example Use Cases**

- **I2C**: Temperature sensors (e.g., LM75), RTCs (e.g., DS1307), OLED displays.
- **SPI**: SD cards, high-speed ADCs/DACs, TFT displays.
